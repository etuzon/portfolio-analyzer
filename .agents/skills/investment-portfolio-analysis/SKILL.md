# Personal Investment Portfolio Analysis

For personal allocation recommendations, first establish goals, horizon, liquidity needs, emergency reserves, debt, loss tolerance and capacity, spending currency, jurisdiction, taxes and existing holdings. When these are unknown, give conditional scenarios and identify the missing facts rather than inventing a suitable allocation. Software analytics can proceed without collecting a personal suitability profile.

Evaluate the whole portfolio, including concentration, overlapping fund exposures, asset classes, currencies, leverage, liquidity, fees and taxes. Distinguish historical measurements, heuristic scores, assumptions and forecasts. Do not present a skill as a professional credential or claim guaranteed returns.

Verify financial guidance, current product facts, costs and tax rules with primary sources. Prefer regulators, official fund documents, exchanges and provider methodology. State the observation period, source, missing coverage and important assumptions alongside analytical conclusions.

For correlation, use percentage returns rather than price levels. Prefer consistently adjusted price histories when available and disclose unadjusted data and native-currency versus investor-currency returns. Never splice raw local prices into an adjusted series. Align identical start/end intervals; do not forward-fill missing prices into zero returns. Deduplicate identical listings across accounts while retaining exchange identity. Require and expose a declared minimum sample; constant series or insufficient overlap produce unavailable results, never zero. Display actual dates and per-pair counts. Pairwise samples can differ and the resulting matrix is not automatically suitable for optimization.

Explain correlation from −1 to +1 as historical linear co-movement. Near zero does not establish independence or safety; correlations can change in a crisis. Asset-return correlations do not reflect the direction or size of a person's long/short exposure. Do not replace a portfolio-risk score with average pairwise correlation.

For performance and risk, separate price return from total return, realized from unrealized results, and time-weighted from money-weighted performance. Account for cash flows and currency conversion explicitly. Avoid annualizing short samples without showing the assumption and uncertainty. Verify financial calculations with independent numerical examples and meaningful missing-data cases.

Useful primary references: [SEC investor guidance on allocation and diversification](https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation), [NIST correlation definition](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman2/auxillar/correlat.htm). Check current guidance when giving advice.
