# Macro Economic Analysis

Define the country, decision, information cutoff, investor currency, and horizon.
Distinguish an economic level, rate of change, surprise versus expectations, and
revision: they answer different questions.

- Read central-bank decisions and official statistical releases before secondary
  commentary. Record release time/timezone, measured period, units, seasonal
  adjustment, annualization, and prior-value revisions.
- For historical analysis use the vintage available at the cutoff. Revised GDP
  or employment cannot stand in for what investors knew earlier.
- Separate nominal/real rates, headline/core inflation, policy/market rates,
  nominal/real FX, and price levels/inflation rates. Identify percent, percentage
  points, and basis points explicitly.
- Evaluate growth, labor, inflation, policy, fiscal stance, yield-curve shape,
  credit spreads, and funding together as relevant. Do not infer a recession or
  policy path from one indicator alone.
- Trace financing costs, discount rates, demand, input costs, currencies, and
  refinancing to exposed companies/holdings. State direction, lag, offsets, and
  missing exposures rather than applying a universal sector sign.
- Distinguish announced policy, implementation, and expectations. Never invent
  pre-release consensus or call news a surprise without expectation evidence.
- Give conditional scenarios where useful. Without an estimated joint model,
  do not multiply related event probabilities or attach precise stock-return
  effects to macro headlines.

Return a dated evidence table, transmission channels, conditional impacts,
alternatives, and the next observable indicators that could change the view.
Use `$portfolio-stress-and-liquidity` for combined holding shocks and
`$quantitative-research-and-model-validation` for effect estimation.
