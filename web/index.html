Open Alarms to review existing Buy, Sell and Watch signals, current portfolio and account risk warnings, and vesting and dividend notices. The header count combines displayed items; repeated matches for the same symbol and signal type count once. Its color reflects the highest attention level present; open the popup for labeled counts and details.

Red marks Sell signals and existing High or Extreme risk scores (61–100); amber marks Watch, Elevated risk (41–60), modeled borrowing, incomplete risk inputs and unconfirmed dividend payments. Green marks complete Buy signals and locally recorded dividend payments; it does not mean an investment is safe. Buy or Watch signals with missing rule or provider inputs are amber; Sell remains red. Blue marks scheduled dividends and purple marks vesting. Text and symbols explain each color.

Missing valuations, quantities, opening cash balances or conversion FX, unfinished or failed analytics updates prevent a complete risk assessment. Limited model inputs retain their existing data-quality penalty. Select a risk row for its current score, contributing factors and missing data; a score is not a probability of loss or a trading instruction.

Color and Type apply together when both are selected. Clearing hides notices without changing holdings, transactions or risk scores. Risk warnings stay hidden for the unchanged assessment and return when its financial inputs change. Signals use the existing dismissal expiry setting; failed dismissals stay visible and can be retried. Clearing requires editing permission; viewers may inspect alarms.

Dividend payment recorded means a local event was processed, not that external broker settlement was verified. Cash posting is shown only with a positive net amount and a recorded transaction. Past pending dates, failed or skipped records and invalid dates remain amber for review; they do not prove nonpayment. Opening Alarms rereads events and saved rule results without placing trades or fetching market quotes. Loading is nonblocking. Investment risk formula

The risk score runs from 0 (very low) to 100 (extreme). It estimates concentration and market exposure; it is not a forecast of the amount you may lose.

Expand Missing data below a risk score to see which securities lack price history, sector or industry, holding currency or asset class, or known economic currency allocations. The value out of 5 is an exposure-weighted component, not a count of missing fields. Each group shows its affected exposure, points before the combined cap, and a recovery step. The disclosure lists every affected symbol; tooltips show a compact category summary. Other missing company-report or dividend fields do not enter this component. Historical details describe the saved observation; correcting current data does not rewrite past observations. Viewers can inspect these explanations; correcting instrument data or classifications requires editing access.

Risk bands: 0–20 low, 21–40 moderate, 41–60 elevated, 61–80 high, and 81–100 extreme.

S = C + A + FX + I + B + D + V + H + IC + Q; risk = clamp(round(S + L), 0, 100)
Concentration · 0–16
Largest-position weight plus the sum of squared position percentages (HHI).
Asset mix · 0–14
Value-weighted asset factors, from 0.05 for cash to 1.00 for options, futures, or leveraged instruments.
Currency exposure · 0–10
Non-base-currency exposure plus the largest single foreign-currency exposure.
Industry concentration · 0–10
Industry HHI contributes up to 7 points and missing industry classifications contribute up to 3.
Cash buffer · 0–7
Five to twenty percent cash adds no risk; lower or unusually high cash adds risk.
Options, futures and leverage · 0–10
Exposure to derivatives or leveraged instruments, plus uncovered shorts.
Volatility · 0–10
Uses the one-year high-low price range when available, otherwise a transparent asset-class proxy.
Investment horizon · 0–10
Uses the shortest linked owner horizon to retirement age; risky assets receive more penalty near retirement.
Issuer and country concentration · 0–8
Measures the largest issuer and unusually concentrated country exposure.
Missing data · 0–5
Adds uncertainty for missing prices, industry, currency, or asset-class data.
Borrowing adjustment
Negative cash is modeled as borrowing. With positive net equity, add base score × borrowing / net equity, capped at the remaining points up to 100. Exposure or borrowing with zero or negative net equity scores 100. This declared heuristic applies to dashboard and simulator risk.

Total portfolio risk is 75% of the combined-holdings score plus 25% of the account scores weighted by gross holdings plus positive cash, rounded and capped to 0–100. Borrowing is summed across accounts, so another account's cash cannot erase it. Combined exposure or borrowing with nonpositive net equity scores 100.

Each industry row recalculates the same component formula for that industry's holdings. Signed portfolio cash and borrowing are allocated in proportion to industry value. Industry scores are comparisons and must not be added together.

Industry names follow the interface language. If a provider industry has no Hebrew translation, a translation-unavailable label appears; switch to English to inspect the original name or assign a supported manual industry.

In Industry split, each security listing appears once within its industry, combining its holdings across accounts. Hover over a symbol to see its exchange, currency, contract details when applicable, and each account’s signed quantity and gross value in the dashboard currency. Different listings, currencies and derivative contracts remain separate. The industry amount and risk still use all underlying holdings. Symbols share the heading row and wrap when space is limited.

In Industry split, select a security to assign or correct its industry locally. Manual classifications override provider data and can be removed later. Bonds always appear under Bonds and are not treated as an industry.

Trading Journal

Only accounts with type Investment are included in this feature. All investment accounts combines those accounts only. ESOP/RSU, Bank and Long-Term Savings accounts are excluded from calculations, coverage and account choices. Shares held in an Investment account remain eligible according to the feature’s asset rules.

Choose a recorded trade from the scrollable list. Several trades remain visible at once; scroll or use the arrow keys to select one and open its reason and reviews.

Open Portfolio → Trading Journal, or choose Journal beside a buy, sell or short transaction in an Investment account in Buy / sell history or Position history details. Select a recorded trade, enter Why and optionally What would change my mind and a Review date, then save. Fully closed positions remain available. Each journal entry links to one whole recorded transaction, including when Position history allocates part of that transaction to a holding period.

The journal preserves the trade facts captured when the entry was created, your first saved reason, and every later reasoning revision with its author and recording time. Recording times show when notes were written; they do not establish that the reason was recorded on the trade date. Saving a revised reason appends history. If the trade is corrected or deleted, the entry remains available with its captured facts and a changed or deleted link status; price evidence is unavailable for that link.

Revisit an entry and add a review rated Good, Mixed or Bad with required review notes or lessons learned. Each review is retained with its author and time; the latest review determines the displayed assessment. Pending means no review has been recorded. Review dates mark pending entries due in the journal. Use account, action and assessment filters and search to find past decisions; dashboard filters do not apply.

Price change since trade uses the latest later saved local price matching the captured symbol, market, asset class and currency. It shows the price date and raw percentage movement from the recorded unit price, independently of your manual assessment. Quotes older than 7 days are marked stale. Missing later prices, unsupported instruments and changed or deleted trades show an explanation. Prices are unadjusted; dividends, fees, taxes and splits can affect interpretation. This is price movement, not realized profit, total return or an automatic Good or Bad rating. Add or correct a later quote in Prices, then reload the journal.

Viewer users can read entries, reasons and review histories. Operators and administrators can save reasons and add reviews. Journal records are saved locally and included in full backups and local data export. Saving journal notes leaves the trade ledger, cash, financial analytics and trade simulations unchanged.

Loading stays inside the journal. Failed saves retain your draft for retry. If another user or tab saved first, reload the latest entry and reconcile your retained draft before saving again; stale revisions cannot overwrite history. Reload reads saved records without downloading market data.

Notes linked to accounts outside the Investment scope remain stored but are hidden here and cannot be edited or reviewed. If a trade moves to an excluded account, its saved notes are also hidden. Eligible notes return when the linked account and trade are within the Investment scope again.

Only accounts with type Investment are included in this feature. All investment accounts combines those accounts only. Open Portfolio → Dividends & Events. The Dividend Calendar tab opens first; choose an account scope and year. The twelve month cards show dates from saved dividend evidence for currently held long stocks and funds. Short positions, ESOP grants and unsupported instruments are excluded. Dashboard filters do not apply.

Each marker shows a distribution amount per share and the stock's observed annual dividend yield. Larger dots indicate larger annual percentages. All ties for the highest comparable yield are highlighted. The comparison always uses the latest twelve months through today, even when viewing an earlier year; it never multiplies one payout by an assumed payment frequency.

The annual summary separates known calendar-year dividends per share, observed last-12-month dividends per share, annual yield, the saved price and its date, and recorded income for that year. Annual yield is last-12-month dividends per share divided by the saved raw closing price, multiplied by 100. Provider-adjusted dividend amounts are used where supplied; special distributions and share splits can affect comparisons. Highest-yield highlighting requires a history record at or before the start of the twelve-month window, a price no older than 7 days and dividend evidence no older than 30 days. Partial or stale values remain visible with warnings.

Calendar dates prefer a reported payment date. If it is missing, the entry explicitly shows an ex-dividend date or an unidentified provider date. These do not establish when cash is received. If no provider distributions have dates in the selected year, recorded dividend transactions for that security supply ledger-date markers instead. Select a marker for its amount, date meaning and annual context. Future dates must already be present in the source; no payment schedule is invented.

Known year totals may include reported future distributions and are not confirmed income received. Recorded year income uses dated local dividend transactions through today, only for currently held eligible securities in the selected account scope; it may be net of tax. Current share quantities are never applied to historical payouts, and currencies are not added together. Missing history or prices are unavailable, not zero.

Dividend data coverage shows source, saved-evidence date, available history dates and excluded or undated record counts. Reload rereads saved evidence without contacting providers or recording dividends and is available to Viewer users. For newer evidence use dashboard Refresh, wait for background completion, then Reload. Loading stays in this dialog; failed requests retain matching saved results and can be retried.

Company Analysis

Open Research → Company Analysis and switch between Company reports and Company outlook. Each tab keeps its existing ticker inputs and results. Portfolio Signals has its own Research menu entry.

Company Reports

Enter a ticker and Get company report to review annual revenue, net income, growth, net margin, free cash flow, and long-term debt when available. Fiscal dates are report year-ends; hover or focus a value for its definition and comparison period.

Reports are cached locally. Fresh data is reused until the configured refresh interval expires; a refresh may download the complete provider dataset rather than only missing years. Saved evidence can remain available if an online refresh fails.

Company Outlook

Analyze a ticker against comparable industry peers. A complete score requires the shared financial measurements and at least two usable peers; incomplete analyses show available evidence without saving a directional forecast. Financial companies require a specialized model. Related industries provide context rather than extra score points.

Scored analyses save their original forecast and report baseline. Check company reports manually compares pending forecasts with newer annual reports using each saved peer group and cutoff. The current evaluation measures revenue growth and net margin versus peers, not stock-price returns. A no-new-report check receives no accuracy grade; failed or incomplete checks can be retried.

Comparison options let you supply peer tickers and related companies. For a historical backtest, choose an as-of date; only reports filed by that date can support the analysis. Historical backtests and calibration runs do not establish prospective forecast accuracy.

Portfolio Signals

Buy, Sell and Watch portfolio alarms evaluate Investment accounts only. ESOP/RSU, Bank and Long-Term Savings accounts do not contribute holdings, allocation weights or condition values.

For Buy, Sell or Watch rules, choose Price from 1Y high, Price from 3Y high or Price from 5Y high and a greater-than or less-than comparison. These signed percentages measure distance from the selected period's high: less than -20% means more than 20% below that high; greater than -20% means closer to it. The 3Y and 5Y measures require sufficient history for that period; partial or missing history is unavailable and cannot satisfy that condition. Other conditions may still match in an Any rule. Saved rules retain their selected period.

Create and enable rules for securities already held, choose whether all or any conditions must match, and save. Rules run daily; Run rules now starts a manual check. Review Active signals and Run history, and open Graph for price context. Matches and account recommendations do not place trades.

Dismiss hides the same stock and signal type across rules for the configured period. Change that period in Settings for new dismissals; existing dismissals keep their original expiry. A failed rule run is different from a successful run with no matches.

Macro Scenarios

Set the Fed raise, cut, and unchanged probabilities to total 100%. Country and global triggers can overlap and do not need to total 100%. Select a trigger, inspect its dependency network, and adjust conditional assumptions; results describe your scenario assumptions, not guaranteed forecasts.

Risk history

Open Analysis & Risk → Risk history. Choose Total portfolio or an account and a period. The line graph uses the last observed score each day on a fixed 0–100 scale; a single observation is a dot and days without observations remain gaps. Scores are heuristic points, not loss probabilities or returns. The dated table provides the saved components and missing-data details.

Recording starts with the first coherent dashboard assessment; past account values cannot reconstruct past risk. An Admin or Operator loading completed portfolio analytics saves an observation automatically. Reload in Risk history retries capture and loads saved observations; Viewers can read saved history. The browser calculates portfolio-risk-v2 from a server-issued snapshot using saved FX; history retains its model, base currency, FX evidence and account IDs. Current badges may differ when they use newer or different FX. Employee equity and ESOP accounts are excluded, and dashboard holdings filters do not alter this scope. Changing data creates a new observation without rewriting earlier records. Value at Risk

When VaR is unavailable, review the listed missing or stale securities and currencies, then expand Portfolio coverage and Historical FX data. The scenario count is evaluated only after coverage checks pass. Refreshing only today’s quotes cannot replace matching historical price and currency series.

Only accounts with type Investment are included in this feature. All investment accounts combines those accounts only. ESOP/RSU, Bank and Long-Term Savings accounts are excluded from calculations, coverage and account choices. Shares held in an Investment account remain eligible according to the feature’s asset rules.

Open Analysis & Risk → Value at Risk. Choose All investment accounts or one investment account and 1, 3 or 5 years of history, then Calculate / Reload. Results and data diagnostics appear only in this dialog. Dashboard filters do not apply. One month means 30 calendar days, and loss is measured at the end of that holding period.

VaR is an estimated 95% loss threshold under a historical model, not an absolute maximum loss or a guarantee. Losses beyond the threshold remain possible in the 5% tail and can be much larger. Average loss in the worst 5% shows expected shortfall. The current recorded net value includes signed security values and cash or borrowing. All amounts in these result cards are in ILS.

Each scenario applies the same observed 30-day native-price and ILS exchange-rate movements to today's recorded positions. For a foreign asset, the value factor is the native-price ratio multiplied by the ILS-per-unit exchange-rate ratio; a falling dollar therefore reduces an unhedged USD position's ILS value even when its USD price is flat. Foreign cash and borrowing also receive the currency movement. The price-only VaR comparison holds FX fixed over the same scenarios; it is not a separate currency-risk contribution that can be added to asset risk.

At least 100 historical scenarios are required, with every selected security price and required exchange rate observed on both dates exactly 30 calendar days apart. Missing dates are never filled and today's FX rate is never substituted for historical movement. Overlapping scenarios are not independent months; fewer than 250 are marked limited history. VaR uses the empirical 95th-percentile loss with nearest-rank selection; expected shortfall averages exactly the worst 5% probability mass, including a fractional boundary scenario. Negative estimated loss thresholds are displayed as zero with a warning that this does not establish zero future risk.

Portfolio coverage lists securities, account cash and borrowing, currencies, values and quote dates. Current security prices and FX observations must be no older than 7 days; the latest common monthly scenario must end within 14 days. Any missing, stale or unsupported position in the selected scope prevents a whole-scope VaR headline. Options, futures, private assets and pension or savings fund balances without supported market-price histories are unavailable. Choose another account or correct the reported evidence. Cash movements recorded in a different currency from their account are unsupported. Use a reconciled account scope with consistent cash currencies; the model does not infer exchange conversions.

FX rates are saved as ILS per one unit of the chosen foreign currency. Editors can select a currency and Record current FX rate to save an actual provider observation dated today. This action does not download past rates. For optional manual history, enter a source and load date,rate CSV, then Save FX history. Dates accept dd/mm/yyyy or yyyy-mm-dd; use positive plain decimal rates, at most 10000 unique dates, and files up to 1 MB. Saving manual history replaces that currency’s local series and recalculates this dialog.

At startup, during background portfolio refresh and in the daily refresh at 23:20 Israel time, the app records actual current rates for required foreign currencies. Already fetched dashboard FX quotes can also be saved. Only observations dated today are accepted; past rates are not downloaded and missing dates, including weekends, are not filled. Each automatic row retains its provider, source and observation time. Only a strictly later provider timestamp can update today’s automatic row. If the provider supplies only a date, the first recorded quote for that date is kept. Manually entered dates remain unchanged, and future observations are added to the saved series. Failed, undated or stale observations leave saved rates intact; background retries wait 5 minutes. Editors can use Record current FX rate to retry immediately, then Calculate / Reload. Recording today’s rate starts building history and does not immediately provide enough matching history for VaR. Calculation itself only reads saved data.

Saved local and qualified cached security prices are used without a market download during calculation. All selected histories use provider adjusted closes only when every asset has complete adjusted evidence and no local override; otherwise every asset uses raw closes. Raw prices can be affected by dividends and splits, and provider adjustments do not guarantee a particular reinvestment method. Maintain missing security history in Prices or through a configured market-data refresh. Representative daily FX fixings and market closes may occur at different times; dated valuations can differ from the dashboard's other quote sources.

Viewer users can calculate and read coverage and saved FX histories; operators and administrators can save or refresh FX evidence. Loading remains local to this dialog. Failed saves and downloads retain entered drafts and saved history. If another tab or user saves first, stale revisions cannot overwrite it; load the latest saved history and reconcile the draft before retrying. FX evidence is included in full backups and local data export. Calculation does not change holdings, account cash or the existing risk score. The model holds current exposures fixed and excludes future trades, deposits, withdrawals, interest, fees, taxes and liquidity effects.

Imported market fields can contain a provider label such as YAHOO. Saved exchange metadata and compatible currency are required to resolve that label before cached prices are used. No exchange is guessed from the provider name; unmatched listings require verified metadata or matching local prices.

Benchmark Comparison

Only accounts with type Investment are included in this feature. All investment accounts combines those accounts only. ESOP/RSU, Bank and Long-Term Savings accounts are excluded from calculations, coverage and account choices. Shares held in an Investment account remain eligible according to the feature’s asset rules.

Open Analysis & Risk → Benchmark Comparison. Choose the entire portfolio or one investment account, the From and To dates, and an index, then Compare. This view uses its own saved performance inputs; dashboard filters do not apply. Viewer users can read and compare; editors can save inputs and refresh supported indexes.

In Performance inputs, enter a source, one reporting currency and dated total values including cash. Use saved account history as a starting point when available, review it, and record every external deposit as positive and withdrawal as negative. Trades, fees and income retained inside the account belong in its value, not in external flows. Transfers between selected accounts are internal; a transfer across the selected scope is an external flow. Confirm the cash-flow history is complete, including when there were no flows, then save. CSV columns are date,value for valuations and date,amount for flows; use dd/mm/yyyy or yyyy-mm-dd, plain numbers without thousands separators, and at most 10000 rows per list.

Valuations are after end-of-day external flows. The opening value already includes flows on the From date; later flows through the To date are included. XIRR is an annualized money-weighted return using actual dates and a 365-day year. TWRR is the cumulative linked return after removing each end-of-day flow; it requires a valuation on every flow date as well as both boundary dates. Missing evidence, incomplete flows, nonpositive bases and potentially ambiguous XIRR cash flows show an explanation instead of a fabricated return. Annualizing less than one year can magnify results. Growth of 100 shows linked returns, so deposits do not create jumps in performance.

The benchmark invests the same opening value and deposits and funds the same withdrawals at the saved index levels. Its XIRR uses those same dated cash flows; its TWRR is the index change over the period. A withdrawal exceeding the simulated benchmark balance makes that comparison unavailable. Index observations use the latest earlier level within 7 days and show the actual observation dates; future observations are never used. Price indexes exclude dividends; total-return series include the reinvestment specified by their source. Review the basis and source before comparing with portfolio returns after actual fees and taxes.

Maintain index history with a name, source, currency, price or total-return basis, and date,level CSV. S&P 500, Nasdaq Composite and Dow Jones can refresh through configured FMP or explicitly enabled Yahoo providers. TA indexes and other indexes support sourced CSV input. TA-25 and TA-100 end on 08/02/2017; select TA-35 or TA-125 for their successors from 09/02/2017. They are separate selections. Portfolio and index series must use the same reporting currency; import a documented currency-converted index series when needed. Current exchange rates are not used to invent historical returns.

Compare reads saved evidence. Saving replaces the selected scope or index inputs locally and includes them in full backups and local data export. Loading and provider refresh stay inside the dialog. Failed saves keep your draft; failed refreshes retain saved history. Retry with Compare or the refresh action after correcting the reported issue. Reload saved inputs to discard a draft and read persisted data.

The combined Investment scope uses new aggregate inputs; previous all-account inputs are preserved but are not reused. Enter valuations and deposits/withdrawals covering only the current Investment accounts. If Investment account membership changes, provide a reconciled aggregate again; individual Investment-account datasets are retained.

Portfolio Optimization

Only accounts with type Investment are included in this feature. All investment accounts combines those accounts only. ESOP/RSU, Bank and Long-Term Savings accounts are excluded from calculations, coverage and account choices. Shares held in an Investment account remain eligible according to the feature’s asset rules.

Open Analysis & Risk → Portfolio Optimization. Choose an account or All investment accounts, one currency and 1, 3 or 5 years of saved history. Select 2 to 30 eligible assets, choose an objective and set a maximum weight per asset, then Optimize. This is a fully invested, long-only model for the selected stocks and funds. Identical listings across accounts are combined. Cash, short positions, derivatives, unsupported assets and other currencies are outside this allocation; the dashboard filters do not apply.

Minimum volatility minimizes estimated portfolio variance. Maximum Sharpe maximizes estimated excess return divided by volatility using the annual risk-free rate you enter; it requires positive feasible excess return and a finite ratio. Target return minimizes variance while meeting at least the entered annual return; a target below the minimum-volatility portfolio return retains that portfolio. Weights total 100%, remain nonnegative and respect the maximum per asset. The cap must be at least 100 divided by the number of selected assets. Infeasible targets or caps and failed numerical checks produce an explanation instead of suggested weights.

Assets and assumptions shows saved price coverage and optional annual expected-return overrides in percent. Blank overrides use the historical estimate. Advanced estimation controls covariance shrinkage: the default 10% moves the sample covariance toward its diagonal; 0% uses the sample matrix and 100% removes estimated cross-asset covariance. This changes the risk model. These are historical estimates and explicit assumptions, not forecasts or guaranteed returns; allocations can be sensitive to the history, assumptions and selected assets.

The model uses the last date with a quote for every selected asset in each completed Monday–Friday week. Every return uses identical start and end dates across all assets. Only intervals of 4 to 10 days are included; longer gaps are skipped, and missing prices are never filled. At least 26 common weekly returns are required; fewer than 52 are marked limited history. Annual expected return is the arithmetic weekly mean multiplied by 52; annual covariance is the sample covariance with denominator n minus 1, multiplied by 52. The selected shrinkage is then applied. Constant-return assets cannot establish a usable risk estimate. Expand the observation details to inspect the actual return intervals.

Provider adjusted closes are used only when every selected asset has complete adjusted evidence and no local overrides. Otherwise all selected series use raw closes, with qualified local overrides. Adjustment scales are never spliced. Provider adjustments do not establish a guaranteed dividend-reinvestment methodology; raw prices can be affected by dividends and splits. The model uses one recorded currency without historical FX conversion. Quotes older than 7 days and common samples ending more than 14 days ago are flagged. For missing history, use a configured dashboard Refresh or maintain qualified prices in Prices, then Reload saved history here.

Cached provider history is accepted for supported US listings recorded in USD and Tel Aviv listings recorded in ILS. Other listings and manually defined instruments without a provider alias require local price history matching their currency and asset class. A market refresh cannot establish that missing identity. Listings with conflicting currencies or asset classes remain unavailable; select one investment account or correct the recorded identity.

Results compare estimated annual return, annual volatility, Sharpe ratio and weights with the current selected sleeve. Current weights use held quantities and latest raw prices no older than 7 days, normalized across only the selected assets; if any selected value is unavailable, that comparison is unavailable. The input table shows current weights across all displayed long holdings in the chosen currency. The efficient frontier shows the minimum-variance portfolio and progressively higher feasible returns. Select a frontier point or its table button to inspect that portfolio; return to the optimized portfolio to restore the chosen objective. Volatility here is a model percentage, separate from the dashboard risk score.

Calculation reads saved evidence and is available to Viewer users as well as editors. It does not place trades, save target holdings or change account balances. Loading and calculation stay inside the dialog. Changing inputs clears the old result; failed calculations retain the inputs for retry with Optimize. Reload saved history rereads local and cached evidence without downloading prices. Fees, taxes, trading costs, liquidity, future market changes and personal suitability are outside this model.

Imported market fields can contain a provider label such as YAHOO. Saved exchange metadata and compatible currency are required to resolve that label before cached prices are used. No exchange is guessed from the provider name; unmatched listings require verified metadata or matching local prices.

Model scope and official mechanism references

The supplied US–Israel chain probabilities are preserved as provided. Sector, commodity, technology, and instrument probabilities are editable assumptions informed by documented transmission mechanisms.

  • Bank of Israel annual report: exchange rate, risk premium, credit, bonds, and funds
  • Federal Reserve: monetary policy and bank net-interest margins
  • BIS: interest-rate exposure and borrower creditworthiness
  • IMF: China property, domestic demand, debt, and trade risks
  • IEA: AI, data centers, electricity, grids, and energy demand
  • World Bank: commodity demand, oil, metals, and gold mechanisms

Portfolio signals

Dismissed signals stay hidden for this many days across all rules for the same stock and signal type. The default is 60 days. Changes apply to new dismissals; existing dismissals keep their original expiry.

Portfolio chat

Keep up to this many chats for your user. When the limit is exceeded, the chat with the oldest last message is removed. Opening a chat does not make it newer. Lowering the limit removes older chats immediately.

Logging

These global settings apply to separate logs for the application, HTTP requests, portfolio, market data, authentication, and chat.

The file limit is per component and includes its active log file. Rotated logs are compressed to ZIP. The oldest archives are deleted when the limit is exceeded.
